On November 19, 2026, the International Court of Justice (ICJ) delivered a landmark verdict, declaring that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority. This historic ruling marks the first instance in which a state has been found guilty of genocide by an international court. This verdict stems from a brutal military crackdown in 2017, resulting in the deaths of thousands and the displacement of over 700,000 Rohingya from Myanmar, formerly known as Burma.

The ICJ, led by President Yuji Iwasawa, concluded that Myanmar acted with 'intent to destroy' a substantial part of the Rohingya population. The court's findings were supported by evidence of 'extensive destruction of evidence' by Myanmar to conceal its crimes. The decision was reached with a majority of nine votes to six from an international panel of judges.

The accusations centered on so-called 'clearance operations' initiated by Myanmar's military in Rakhine State. In 2017, following attacks by the Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army (Arsa) on police posts, Myanmar's military, aided by local Buddhist mobs, responded with overwhelming violence, prompting the mass exodus of the Rohingya. Survivors recounted harrowing tales of entire villages being razed and widespread sexual violence targeting women and girls.

The evidence presented to the court, including testimonies from survivors, suggested approximately 10,000 Rohingya were 'violently killed' and identified more than 700,000 fleeing to Bangladesh. The total number targeted during these operations is estimated to be around 815,000.

While the court recognized that Arsa played a role in the conflict, it found that a prevailing 'anti-Rohingya hate speech campaign' significantly overlapped with the military's clearance operations, enhancing the likelihood of violence against the minority group.

Myanmar has consistently denied the allegations of genocide, claiming its military operations were aimed at neutralizing militant threats from insurgent groups. Myanmar officials maintain that the operations were a legitimate response to Arsa's attacks, a stance reiterated by government representative Ko Ko Hlaing during proceedings.

Despite Myanmar's denials, the ICJ found compelling evidence to support 'genocidal intent,' necessitating the country's accountability under international law. Beyond the immediate verdict, Myanmar has been advised to provide reparations, although a specific sum is yet to be determined.

The ruling has been hailed as a 'formal recognition' of suffering by both Rohingya communities and global human rights organizations. It represents a significant step towards justice for the Rohingya, who have faced systemic discrimination, including policies intending to erode their rights.

The ICJ's decision underscores a pivotal moment for international accountability and human rights advocacy. As UN human rights chief Volker Türk noted, the verdict must translate into tangible action to address the 'suffering, misery, and humiliation' inflicted on the Rohingya people.
