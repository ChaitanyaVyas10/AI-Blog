Tens of thousands of Spaniards took to the streets in over 50 protests across major cities including Madrid, Valencia, and Barcelona, voicing their frustration over Spain's escalating housing crisis. These demonstrations, which occurred on October 3, 2026, were sparked by the government's failure to enact emergency housing legislation designed to address affordability and eviction concerns.

The tipping point for public outrage was the eviction of an 87-year-old woman, Maricarmen Abascal, which drew significant media attention and emotional responses nationwide. The protests were organized by a coalition of housing rights groups, with the Tenants' Union estimating up to 500,000 attendees in Madrid alone. However, official estimates were more conservative, citing approximately 70,000 participants, illustrating the uncertainty around the scale of support.

Central to the protesters' demands were calls for reduced rents, cessation of evictions, and restrictions on property acquisitions by investment funds, which are believed to exacerbate the crisis by inflating property prices and reducing the availability of affordable housing. The government's proposed, but ultimately unpassed, decrees aimed to temporarily suspend the eviction of vulnerable tenants until 2030 and extend rental leases, measures seen by many as crucial interim relief.

Despite the rejection of the legislative package in Congress on October 1, there remains uncertainty regarding how Prime Minister Sánchez's administration will navigate this widespread discontent. Future political maneuvers or potential early elections could significantly impact Spain's housing policy landscape.
